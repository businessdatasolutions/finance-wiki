# Edward I. Altman

Professor Emeritus of Finance at the **Stern School of Business, New York University**. The founder of statistical bankruptcy prediction in finance, author of the original **[[altman-z-score]]** (1968) and its successors (Zeta 1977; Z′ 1995; Z′′ 1995; international 2017; **[[omega-score]]** 2023).

## Contributions

- **1968** — *Financial Ratios, Discriminant Analysis and the Prediction of Corporate Bankruptcy*, Journal of Finance 23(4) 589–609. The founding paper of statistical bankruptcy prediction. Introduced the five-ratio Z-score using [[multiple-discriminant-analysis]] on US manufacturing firms.
- **1977** — *ZETA Analysis: A New Model to Identify Bankruptcy Risk of Corporations* (with Haldeman & Narayanan), Journal of Banking & Finance 1(1) 29–54. Refined 27-indicator extension.
- **1995** — *Z′* and *Z′′* variants for non-listed and non-manufacturing firms.
- **2007** — *Modelling Credit Risk for SMEs* (with Sabato), Abacus 43(3) 332–357. First SME-targeted adaptation.
- **2017** — *Financial Distress Prediction in an International Context: A Review and Empirical Analysis of Altman's Z-Score Model* (with Iwanicz-Drozdowska, Laitinen, Suvas), Journal of International Financial Management & Accounting 28(2) 131–171. International validation of Z′′.
- **2023** — [[omega-score]] paper extending Z′′ with management + employee + payment behaviour variables on Croatian SMEs.

## Significance for the wiki

The Z-score lineage Altman founded is the **canonical accounting-based distress-prediction tradition**. Every paper in the 2026-05-25 batch either (a) extends the lineage (Altman 2023's Omega; Powell 2024's ASEAN MDA), (b) catalogues it (Habib 2020's Table 1), or (c) compares against it (Hajek 2024's XGBOD AUC 0.9864 implicitly benchmarks against the Altman tradition; Bari 2026's financial-only baseline R² 0.31 effectively measures the Z-score channel's contribution alone).

Even [[2012-09-01-luppe-2012-anchoring-accounting-indicators|Luppe 2012]] — the behavioural outlier — refers indirectly to Altman in the broader landscape of accounting-judgment research.
