# Table 5 — Frequency of ratios appearing in discriminant functions

> The aggregate count of how often each of Powell's 14 candidate ratios survived stepwise regression and entered an ASEAN-region discriminant function. Counts are across the **6 country-models × 7 periods + 7 region-models = up to 49 opportunities per ratio**. EBIT/TA (X₁) leads with 33 entries; FU/TL (X₁₂) and MVE/TC (X₁₃) appear least often (5 and 2 entries). This table is the **empirical synthesis** of [[powell-2024-prior-literature-matrix|the prior-literature matrix]] (Table 1) — which ratios survived re-testing on the ASEAN sample.

## Headline rank

1. **X₁ EBIT/TA** — 33 entries (out of 49 maximum). The single most reliable ASEAN distress predictor across both countries and periods.
2. **X₄ RE/TA** — 15. Retained earnings is the second-most-frequent profitability proxy.
3. **X₃ NI/TA** — 13. Net-income margin is the third-ranked profitability ratio.
4. **X₂ S/TA, X₁₀ NOCREDINT** — 12 each. Asset turnover (sales efficiency) and the no-credit interval (a liquidity proxy) tie for fourth.
5. **X₅ EBITDA/TL, X₆ WC/TA, X₉ CL/TA, X₁₁ TL/TA** — 11 each. The mid-tier ratios — each significant in roughly a quarter of country×period combinations.
6. **X₈ CA/CL** — 7. The classical current ratio appears less often than the alternative liquidity measures (WC/TA, NOCREDINT).
7. **X₁₄ MVE/TL** — 6. Market-value-of-equity / total-liabilities. The Altman et al. 1977 indicator.
8. **X₁₂ FU/TL, X₇ CA/TL, X₁₃ MVE/TC** — 5, 3, 2. The least reliable ratios in the ASEAN MDA context.

## What the rank means

The first four positions (EBIT/TA, RE/TA, NI/TA, S/TA + NOCREDINT) are all **profitability or asset-efficiency** ratios — i.e. ASEAN distress in this sample is signalled primarily through low or negative profitability rather than through liquidity or leverage. This is consistent with the descriptive-statistics gaps in [[powell-2024-descriptive-statistics]] where the largest distressed–non-distressed differences are on profitability (EBIT/TA, RE/TA) rather than on liquidity (CA/CL, NOCREDINT). Leverage ratios are more period-dependent — TL/TA is significant mainly in crisis periods (AFC, GFC, Post-GFC).
