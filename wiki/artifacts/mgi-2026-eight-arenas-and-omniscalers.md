# MGI 2026 — eight competitive arenas + nine omniscalers

> The named framework from the McKinsey Global Institute's May 2026 report *The race takes off in the next big arenas of competition*. **Eight industries** where structural-competitive fluidity creates outsized growth (and outsized destruction); **nine "omniscaler" companies** that compete meaningfully across multiple arenas at unprecedented capex scale. Sourced from the [[2026-05-26-mckinsey-2026-next-big-arenas-of-competition|May 12 launch webcast]]; the underlying MGI report has not yet been ingested into the wiki — this artifact reproduces the framework at webcast-level fidelity.

## The eight arenas

Verbatim from the webcast description:

| # | Arena | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI services** | Generative AI as a service: foundation models, agent platforms, fine-tuning, inference. The arena MGI describes as the **AI foundation** that other arenas build on. |
| 2 | **Semiconductors** | Chip stack underpinning AI services — design + manufacturing + packaging + advanced lithography. ASML, TSMC, NVIDIA all central. |
| 3 | **Cloud infrastructure** | Hyperscale compute and storage platforms — AWS, Azure, GCP, plus the Chinese hyperscalers. |
| 4 | **Advanced manufacturing** | AI-enabled manufacturing; reshoring + supply-chain reconfiguration; precision robotics integration. |
| 5 | **Robotics** | Industrial robotics + humanoid robotics (the latter accelerating notably since 2024). |
| 6 | **Next-generation energy systems** | SMRs (small modular reactors), grid-scale storage, fusion investment, and the renewables-plus-AI-load infrastructure stack. |
| 7 | **Space** | Commercial space economy: satellite constellations, launch economics, in-orbit services. |
| 8 | **Biopharma** | AI-discovered drugs, the GLP-1 (Ozempic-family) wave, gene/cell therapies. |

### Cross-arena structure

Per MGI's framing in the webcast:

- **Arenas 1–3 (AI services, semiconductors, cloud) form a "stack"** — value creation in AI services depends on capacity built in cloud, which depends on capacity built in semiconductors.
- **Arenas 4–5 (advanced manufacturing + robotics) are tightly coupled** — modern advanced-manufacturing competitiveness increasingly requires robotics integration.
- **Arenas 6–8 are more independent** — next-gen energy, space, biopharma each have their own structural drivers though all benefit from the AI-foundation layer.

## The nine omniscalers

**Note: the exact list of 9 firms was referenced throughout the webcast but not enumerated in a clean single segment that the wiki ingest captured.** From context cues across the discussion, the omniscalers appear to include the US Big Tech hyperscalers (some subset of Microsoft, Google/Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, NVIDIA, Apple) plus likely Chinese counterparts (Alibaba, Tencent, etc.). The webcast moderator (Chris Bradley) refers to "the 9 omniscalers" specifically and several panelists agree the list is empirically tight.

### Defining characteristics of omniscalers (extractable from the webcast)

1. **Compete meaningfully in 3+ arenas simultaneously.**
2. **Invest at unprecedented capex scale** — described in the discussion as "larger than mid-sized countries' GDP" annually.
3. **Build cross-arena moats** that single-arena specialists cannot match — talent pool, capex-financing capacity, data assets, distribution networks.
4. **AI as the cross-arena bridge** — the omniscalers are the firms whose AI capabilities can be deployed across multiple arenas, multiplying returns to AI investment.

## What the 2026 update emphasises (vs the 2024 MGI arenas iteration)

The 2024 MGI report (predecessor to this 2026 update) introduced the arenas concept and named an earlier list. The 2026 update emphasises:

- **AI as the foundation layer.** The 2024 arenas list pre-dated the post-ChatGPT explosion; the 2026 update positions AI services + semiconductors + cloud as the central "stack."
- **Race velocity.** The 2026 framing is that the *gap between leaders and laggards is widening* — competition is no longer about entry but about leader-lap-laggard distance.
- **Capex scale shift.** The 2026 omniscalers are investing capex orders of magnitude beyond what 2020 hyperscalers spent.
- **Geographic concentration.** US (West Coast) and China dominate; Europe is meaningful only in **semiconductors** (ASML, IMEC), **biopharma** (Novo Nordisk, Roche), and **selectively** in robotics + next-gen energy.

## What this artifact is NOT

- **Not the authoritative omniscaler list** — that lives in the MGI report, not yet ingested.
- **Not portable across time** — the 8 arenas are 2026-specific. The 2024 list was different; the 2028 list will be different again. Arenas churn is the point.
- **Not predictive at the firm level.** MGI names the present winners; whether the omniscaler pattern *predicts* future winners (a forward claim) vs *describes* present winners (a descriptive claim) is not separable from the launch-event content.
- **Not a complete competitive-strategy framework.** MGI's arenas concept is one lens among several; Porter's Five Forces, Christensen disruption theory, and resource-based-view frameworks all overlap with arenas at different angles.
