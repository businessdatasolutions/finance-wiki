## Headline findings by panel

### Firm-fundamental determinants panel (~30 author rows)

Surveyed papers consistently find:

- **Profitability** (especially ROA, EBIT/TA) is the strongest predictor across studies.
- **R&D investment** has *mixed* effects — Zhang 2015 finds ↑ distress; other studies find ↓ distress via innovation lift. Resolution: the sign depends on life-cycle stage.
- **CSR** has small but significant ↓ distress effects, mediated by reputation and supplier-relationship channels.
- **Operating efficiency** measures (cost-margin compression, asset-turnover decline) reliably predict distress 2–3 years out.

### Macro-economic determinants panel (~20 author rows)

- **Industry concentration** has ambiguous effects — concentrated industries shelter incumbents but punish entrants.
- **Recession × leverage interaction** is the dominant macro channel: high-leverage firms in recessions face disproportionate distress risk.
- **Monetary-policy regime** matters: distress rates rise sharply when interest rates rise faster than firm pricing power.

### Corporate-governance determinants panel (~30 author rows)

- **Board independence** has mixed effects — independent directors ↓ distress in stable contexts, but Hsu-Wu 2014 finds grey directors > independent in distress prediction.
- **CEO characteristics**: long-tenure ↓ distress (institutional knowledge); CEO with past-default history ↑ corporate bankruptcy risk substantially (Kallunki-Pyykko 2013).
- **Ownership structure**: family ownership ↓ distress; blockholder concentration mixed.
- **Audit-committee quality** ↓ distress in samples with strong external audit-quality indicators.
