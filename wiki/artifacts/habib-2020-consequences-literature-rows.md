## Headline findings by panel

### Financial reporting & auditing consequences panel (~25 author rows)

The largest panel — captures how distress changes accounting behaviour:

- **Earnings management** (real and accrual) increases as distress approaches; distressed firms shift earnings forward to delay covenant breach.
- **Classification shifting** (moving items between operating and non-operating lines) rises in pre-distress windows — Habib et al. cite multiple studies finding the misclassified items consistently make operating income look healthier.
- **Audit-quality response**: Big-4 auditors charge higher fees for distressed clients (audit-risk premium); going-concern opinion frequency rises 2–3 years before formal distress filing.
- **Restatement frequency** rises both before and after distress — pre-distress restatements reflect management's earnings-management attempts unwinding; post-distress restatements reflect the new auditor's "house cleaning."

### Firm-level operational consequences panel (~20 author rows)

- **Dividend cuts** are the most-studied operational consequence — sharply higher distress-period dividend-cut probability across many country contexts.
- **Capital-expenditure reductions** of 20–40% (sample-conditional) in the 2 years pre-distress, with persistent under-investment effects post-distress survival.
- **Workforce reductions** lag distress by ~12 months; layoff size correlates with distress severity.
- **Asset sales and divestitures** rise sharply in distressed firms; fire-sale discount of 15–30% on average vs. comparable arms-length transactions.

### Capital market consequences panel (~15 author rows)

- **Stock-return predictability**: distressed firms exhibit lower future returns (the "distress anomaly") — extensive literature on whether this reflects mispricing or risk premium.
- **Accrual-anomaly pricing**: the accrual anomaly is stronger in distressed-firm samples; market under-prices accruals at the distress-tier extreme.
- **Analyst forecast accuracy** declines for distressed firms; forecast dispersion rises; analyst coverage drops by 20–30% in 2 years pre-distress.
- **Credit-rating downgrades** are mechanically tied to distress definition but Habib catalogues the cascade effects (covenant breaches, bond-spread blow-out, equity-issuance cost spike).

### Corporate governance consequences panel (~20 author rows)

- **CEO turnover** rises ~2–3× in distressed firms vs. matched controls; the turnover effect is stronger when distress is performance-attributable (vs. macro-driven).
- **Board restructuring** follows distress — typical pattern is independent-director additions + CEO replacement within 18 months of distress diagnosis.
- **Blockholder activism** rises; distressed firms attract higher activist-investor entry rates.
- **M&A activity**: distressed firms are 4–5× more likely to be takeover targets in the next 24 months.
