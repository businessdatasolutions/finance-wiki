# 50 Founders Share How They Got Their First Customers

> At the start of the S23 batch we interviewed 50 founders about how they managed to get their very first customers. It's a snapshot in time that highlights the different tactics YC founders employ in their earliest stages.

## TL;DR

Y Combinator montage video — 4 minutes, **16 founder responses** edited together (the title's "50 founders" refers to the original event sample, but the published video features 16 named startups). Each founder answers "how did you get your first customer?" in 5-20 seconds. The aggregate pattern: **network-and-outreach dominates** (founders' personal/extended networks; cold email; LinkedIn; door-to-door; advisor introductions) with viral-launch as a high-variance outlier (the Reworkd AI case: Twitter launch → 50 to 10k followers, 20k GitHub stars in days). Reproduced as the wiki's first **go-to-market** source; pairs naturally with the later academic / consulting GTM literature future ingests will add.

## Context (WHY)

Y Combinator's S23 (Summer 2023) batch was active during the post-ChatGPT, pre-mass-LLM-saturation wave. The video documents how that cohort's founders handled the universal early-stage question: **how do you get your first paying customer when no one knows you exist?**

The "50 founders" of the title refers to the original survey at the start of the batch; the published montage shows 16 (the rest were presumably cut for length or for response quality). YC's editorial framing: a snapshot of tactic-frequency rather than prescription.

## Methods (HOW)

YC's editorial process — surveyed 50 founders at start of S23 batch; edited 16 responses into a 4-minute montage. No claim is made that the 16 are representative of the 50, but the 16 cover a reasonable range of tactics.

## Results (WHAT)

The 16 founder responses, paraphrased into channel-category groupings:

### Network-based (~5 founders)

- **Ohmic Biosciences** — *"one really good advisor who sort of made some introductions to folks at a couple of the big AG tech companies; sort of networking our way in."*
- **Accend** — *"first customer we got was a friend of ours; hitting up friends; talking to other people in the space."*
- **Craftwork** — *"we launched with friends and family so that way we can test our processes out, our pricing... then it kind of naturally evolved; we started getting referrals."*
- **Helios** — *"trice's extended family holiday home pages to put in the first system."*
- **Letter AI** — *"as with a lot of founders, your first few customers come from your network; I was fortunate enough to work with some amazing folks across a wide range of industries; went and told them what we were building, they liked it enough to put real money behind it."*

### Cold outreach (~4 founders)

- *"surprisingly, a cold email"* (founder not named in chapters)
- *"a lot of calls and emailing on Reddit"*
- *"the first one was actually just kind of very crafted cold outreach"*
- *"got our first customer by using LinkedIn"* (Platform)

### In-person hustle (~2 founders)

- *"got our first customer just literally going door to door, standing in the sun waiting for the owner to just show up — he was in his food truck all day."*
- **Kino AI** — *"immediately after I get out of here I'm getting on a plane to go to LA for a few days, and we're going to make the rounds of the production companies."*

### Voicemail / phone follow-up (~1 founder)

- **Shasta Health** — *"I'd left a voicemail for one of our physical therapists; she called me back; she was like 'tell me more about you know what got your start'; we actually gave our background on like this is why we got into it; she thought it was like we were coming from a good place."*

### Customer-discovery-to-first-sale (~1 founder)

- **Kapa.ai** — *"just customer discovery calls to try and figure out if this was something people would be interested in; we sold it to one company for $250 for a month; we didn't have any code; we just made like a version of it in seven days and deployed it."*

### Community-post + waitlist (~2 founders)

- **Envelope Money** — *"made a mock-up of our dream personal finance product; made a website for it with a little waitlist; posted it on our Reddit group; within 24 hours there was like close to a thousand people that had signed up."*
- **Greenlite** — *"we launched, we shared what we were doing about two and a half weeks ago on just our LinkedIns and shared with our friends; got a great waitlist."*

### Viral product launch (~1 founder — high variance outlier)

- **Reworkd AI** — *"it was a bit of a wild week in which we launched on Twitter and suddenly a few days later I went from 50 followers to like 10,000; we had 20,000 stars on GitHub; we had 150,000 unique visitors a day."*

### Reverse-recruiter exploit (~1 founder, novel tactic)

- **MantleBio** — *"I take all the companies that message me trying to hire me as a software engineer for their biotech company and I respond saying 'no but how would you like to buy software that does the same thing.'"*

### Aggregate frequency pattern

| Channel category | Founders | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Network-based (friends / family / advisor intros) | ~5 | Most common single category |
| Cold outreach (email / Reddit / LinkedIn) | ~4 | Second-most-common |
| Community-post + waitlist | ~2 | High-leverage when audience matches problem |
| In-person hustle (door-to-door / sales trips) | ~2 | Worked for SMB / location-bound customers |
| Customer-discovery-to-first-sale | ~1 | The Lean Startup pattern in miniature |
| Voicemail / phone follow-up | ~1 | One of the more memorable specific stories |
| Reverse-recruiter exploit | ~1 | Novel inversion of inbound-recruiter messages |
| Viral product launch | ~1 | High-variance — works rarely, spectacularly when it does |

## Discussion / Significance (SO WHAT)

For the wiki:

1. **First go-to-market source.** The wiki's corpus has heavy coverage of financial-distress detection + response + prevention, but until now zero content on the upstream question of how firms acquire their first customers. This source seeds that direction.
2. **Network-channel dominance.** The 5-of-16 ratio for network-based first-customer acquisition is consistent with the broader VC-literature finding that early-stage GTM is network-mediated more than channel-mediated. A future ingest of a more rigorous study (e.g. First Round's *State of Startups* annual reports) would let this concept be properly developed.
3. **Adjacent to [[mission-protection-via-governance]] via the founder-control theme.** Ries's framework presupposes founders who have built something worth protecting; this video documents the *origin* of that something — the moment a startup acquires its first paying user. Future syntheses on the founder lifecycle could connect these.

**Limitations the source acknowledges:** YC explicitly frames the video as a snapshot, not as a representative survey.

**Limitations not flagged:**

- **Selection on YC startups.** YC's batch composition is its own selection bias — these are pre-vetted founders who already passed YC's interview gate. Their first-customer tactics are unlikely to generalise to founders outside that pipeline.
- **Selection on outcomes.** The video features S23 founders who survived to be filmed; failed founders' tactics are not in the sample.
- **Self-reported recall.** Founders describe what they remember worked; the tactics that didn't work are not surveyed.
- **Tactic-frequency ≠ tactic-effectiveness.** The 5-of-16 network-channel dominance doesn't tell us whether network-channel founders had higher revenue 12 months later. That comparison would require YC's outcome data, not the launch-tactic data.
