# What great leaders know about not knowing it all

> People often expect their leaders to have all the answers; David Novak built his career by challenging that assumption. On this episode of *The McKinsey Podcast*, the former CEO and cofounder of Yum! Brands and current chair of Versant speaks with Eric Kutcher, McKinsey's chair of North America, about the experiences that shaped his leadership — from engaging with front-line franchisees to managing a very public product flop — and how other leaders can learn from them.

## Results (WHAT)

Novak's substantive leadership claims, distilled from the interview:

### Crystal Pepsi as failure case study

- The 1992 Crystal Pepsi launch failed because it "didn't taste enough like Pepsi" — i.e. consumers wanted a Pepsi-flavoured alternative, not a different drink that happened to be clear.
- Novak's specific self-criticism (~15:39 mark): *"I didn't listen to them [people closest to the product]. The other thing was, I rushed it into market..."*
- The failure becomes the structural argument for the rest of Novak's leadership method: not listening to the people closest to the product/customer is the canonical executive failure mode.

### Asking questions as the leadership method

Novak's core principle: **the best leaders ask questions; they don't pretend to have all the answers**. Repeated multiple times across the interview. Operational implications:

- Engage front-line workers directly (franchisees, store managers, baristas, etc.) rather than relying on filtered executive reporting.
- Treat your unknowns as the information-richest places in your day — not the places to avoid.
- The best CEOs Novak has worked with all share this disposition; the worst all believed they had the answers already.

### Recognition as the single greatest behaviour-change lever

Closing argument (~21:17–22:17): *"Recognition... is probably the single greatest way to change behavior."* Novak repeats this as the load-bearing principle of his leadership method — recognition compounds when delivered specifically and publicly, in a way that performance management without recognition does not.

### "Take Charge of You" undertone

Several segments echo themes from Novak's 2022 book *Take Charge of You* (co-written with Jason Goldsmith). The book's central premise is self-coaching as a leadership capability; the podcast doesn't explicitly cite the book but the framing aligns.

**Limitations not flagged:**
