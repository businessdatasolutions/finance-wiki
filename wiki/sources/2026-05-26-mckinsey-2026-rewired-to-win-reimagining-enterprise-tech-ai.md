# Rewired to win: Reimagining the enterprise with tech and AI

> As headlines with big claims about AI dominate the news, leaders are trying to realize the promise of these technologies. A set of outperforming companies prove that a specific constellation of behaviors—including focus, strong underlying capabilities, and the right approach to change—can help leaders translate new technologies into real, meaningful value.

## Context (WHY)

The McKinsey book *Rewired: The McKinsey Guide to Outcompeting in the Age of Digital and AI* (Eric Lamarre, Kate Smaje, Rodney Zemmel; Wiley, 2023) is McKinsey's flagship digital-transformation publication. This 2026 webinar revisits the book's framework in the context of the post-2023 generative-AI wave — i.e. does the *Rewired* methodology still hold three years after publication, now with agentic AI in the mix?

The framing tension the session opens with: AI headlines are saturated with big claims; meanwhile most enterprise AI deployments are underperforming relative to those claims. The book argues that the *value gap* between AI-claims and AI-outcomes is not a technology problem but an *operating-model* problem — companies that have built the capabilities the book names ("rewired" their operating model) are translating AI into value; companies that haven't are not.

## Results (WHAT)

The webinar's substantive content centres on **what outperformer firms do differently**. From the description: a "specific constellation of behaviors — including **focus, strong underlying capabilities, and the right approach to change**." The book's underlying framework (per public McKinsey publications) names six capabilities:

1. **Strategy** — vision for what the rewired enterprise looks like.
2. **Talent** — engineering, design, product, data talent at sufficient density.
3. **Operating model** — distributed teams aligned to value-creating outcomes.
4. **Technology** — modern modular tech stack supporting iteration speed.
5. **Data** — high-quality, accessible data products treated as assets.
6. **Adoption + change management** — scaling validated solutions across the organisation.

(The six capabilities are inferred from the book's public materials; the webinar does not enumerate them as a clean list — they're discussed conversationally.)

The 2026 refresh emphasises:

- **AI specifically (not just digital).** The 2023 book leaned on "digital and AI" jointly; the 2026 conversation tilts toward AI as the dominant driver of the value gap.
- **Agentic AI.** Smaje signals (~22 min mark) interest in agentic AI as a coming wave the framework will need to adapt to.
- **Focus as the under-appreciated capability.** The session repeats the point that most companies dilute their AI investment across too many use cases — the outperformers pick a small number of high-value domains and go deep.

## Discussion / Significance (SO WHAT)

For the wiki:

1. **The first AI-enterprise-transformation source in the corpus.** Adjacent to but distinct from [[corporate-turnaround]] (which focuses on turning around distressed firms) — *Rewired* is about transformation of healthy-but-stagnant firms toward AI-leveraged value capture. A future concept page [[ai-enterprise-transformation]] could anchor on this source plus future book + research ingests.
2. **McKinsey's own positioning on AI.** Given McKinsey's market footprint, their official framing of "what outperformer firms do with AI" is a corpus-relevant baseline regardless of methodological rigour. Cross-reference against future Bain / BCG / industry-specific frameworks.
3. **Adjacent to [[2026-05-24-erginbilgic-2026-rolls-royce-turnaround-playbook|Erginbilgiç's]] four-pillar playbook.** Both are multi-dimensional change frameworks. Erginbilgiç emphasises pillars 1-4 (people / strategy / contracts / performance management) at firm-distress level; *Rewired* emphasises capabilities at firm-strategy-execution level. Encoded as a typed `supports` relationship; the prose connection is that both reject single-dimensional interventions.

**Limitations the source acknowledges:** the webinar is promotional for the book; selection bias on the outperformer firms ("we studied the winners and they did X") is the canonical methodological objection McKinsey faces and the webinar does not engage it.

**Limitations not flagged:**

- **Reverse causation on outperformer behaviours.** The book identifies firms that outperformed AND had certain behaviours. The causal direction (behaviours → outperformance vs outperformance → behaviours) is not established.
- **The book is from 2023.** Some of the recommendations may be obsolete in the agentic-AI era of 2026. The webinar acknowledges this in passing but doesn't update the framework systematically.
- **No quantitative outcome data.** AUC-style accuracy of the framework's predictive power is not reported; the case for the framework rests on McKinsey's expert credibility rather than measurable forecast performance.
