# Rolls-Royce CEO Tufan Erginbilgiç Shares His Turnaround Playbook

> How do you turn around a struggling industrial giant? On this episode of Leaders With Francine Lacqua, Rolls-Royce CEO Tufan Erginbilgiç explains the decisions, mindset shifts and leadership principles behind the UK enginemaker's comeback — and why he believes British businesses must be willing to challenge old habits to stay competitive.

## TL;DR

Tufan Erginbilgiç, Rolls-Royce CEO since January 2023, articulates a **four-pillar turnaround playbook** — people/leadership/vision, granular strategy, contract renegotiation, performance management — that he frames as *transformation* (culture + business-model change) rather than *restructuring* (cost cutting). His widely-reported "burning platform" speech is positioned as a **data-grounded mirror** (External Benchmarking commissioned September 2022, before he formally started) rather than tough-love rhetoric. Stock up ~10× under his tenure; McKinsey case-study designation. The wiki's first corporate-turnaround primary source — connects the *response phase* to the distress phenomena the academic papers in this corpus document.

## Context (WHY)

Erginbilgiç took over Rolls-Royce in January 2023, inheriting a company described in the interview as having: shares down ~70% during his predecessor's tenure; over a decade of underperformance; balance sheet "in terrible shape"; cost of capital significantly higher than returns; no clear strategy or performance management; battered by COVID. Within months he delivered the *burning platform* speech to employees — internally, not as a press conference — that became the symbolic kick-off of the turnaround.

By the time of this 2026-05-24 interview, the share price had risen by more than 10× under his tenure. The McKinsey case-study designation lends external validation. The interview sits within Bloomberg's *Leaders with Francine Lacqua* franchise, which positions itself as primary-source CEO testimony rather than analyst interpretation.

The wiki's existing corpus has documented the *detection* side of corporate distress (Altman Z-score, Omega Score, Hajek BERTopic, Powell ASEAN MDA, Habib determinants/consequences review, Bari behavioural framework). This source opens a new layer — the *response* side: what does a CEO actually do when the distress models say "yes, this firm is in trouble"?

## Methods (HOW)

Not applicable in the academic sense — this is qualitative practitioner testimony, not a study. The interview's *methodological content* is what Erginbilgiç describes as his approach:

- **Pre-arrival diagnostic.** Commissioned External Benchmarking starting September 2022 (four months before he formally started). The benchmarking data is what made the burning-platform speech "data-grounded" rather than a critique.
- **Internal-first communication.** The burning-platform speech was a 1.5-hour internal town hall, not a press conference. Headlines came from the first ten minutes only.
- **Layer elimination, not operational cuts.** "No operational people left" — the thousands of job cuts targeted management layers and overhead, not engineering/manufacturing workforce.
- **Workshop-based strategy development.** 25–30 strategy workshops with 500+ people total participation. Erginbilgiç personally attended all of them. Rule he stated on entering each room: "no hierarchy; this will be chaotic."
- **Contract renegotiation via CEO-to-CEO escalation.** Counterparty negotiations conducted at the CEO-of-Rolls-Royce ↔ CEO-of-counterparty level given numbers involved.
- **360-degree performance management** tied to reward and recognition, with growth as the enabling condition (shrinking companies create fewer career opportunities, feeding a doom loop).

## Results (WHAT)

The interview presents these self-reported outcomes (verifiable against external sources, though not done here):

- **Share price up >10×** under Erginbilgiç's tenure (Jan 2023 → May 2026).
- **McKinsey case study** designation as "the art of corporate transformation."
- **"Resilient" response to 2025 tariff shocks** — Erginbilgiç claims the company couldn't have responded to current external shocks three years ago ("probably 10% of what we are doing"). Attributes the difference to the mindset + capability built during the transformation.
- **Cultural change** — explicit claim that "the company moved to a new normal" where the CEO no longer needs to push pace; "team is behaving very differently."

Specific claims about the turnaround mechanism:

- **Distinction between restructuring and transformation.** Erginbilgiç insists the company underwent transformation, not (yet another) restructuring — and that this framing distinction matters to employees who had seen multiple restructurings before.
- **The four pillars** (people / strategy / contracts / performance management) are presented as necessary jointly, not individually.
- **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)** mentioned as the kind of strategic bet enabled by the turnaround — Rolls-Royce SMR positioned for European energy security + net-zero needs, advantaged by 85% manufacturing process (vs. bespoke EPC nuclear projects).

### Resilience-before-crisis playbook (inline — short)

```
Pre-crisis preparation:
1. Mindset (most important)   — develop the response disposition before trouble
2. Capability                  — the operational ability to act on the mindset
3. Agility                     — using the capability under pressure
4. Action-orientation          — "action oriented, not explanation oriented"
                                 — because you can't know in advance what the trouble is

Application example (Erginbilgiç's claim):
  2025 tariff shocks → Rolls-Royce responded to ~100% of what's needed
  Three years prior (~2022) → would have responded to ~10%
  Difference attributed to the mindset+capability built during 2023-2025 transformation
```

## Discussion / Significance (SO WHAT)

For the wiki, three contributions land:

1. **First primary-source CEO-turnaround testimony in the corpus.** All five academic papers document the *detection* of distress (predictor variables, classification accuracy, NLP signals, taxonomies of determinants/consequences). This source documents the *response* — what an actual CEO actually did. Future ingests can build out the response side with consulting case studies, restructuring-practitioner books, and additional CEO interviews.

2. **The transformation-vs-restructuring distinction is methodologically operational.** Erginbilgiç's framing is that restructuring is cost cutting (a one-dimensional financial intervention) while transformation is culture + business-model change (multi-dimensional). The four pillars give the distinction operational content. For wiki readers cross-referencing the [[financial-distress]] literature, this is a useful taxonomic boundary: when the distress-prediction models say a firm is in trouble, transformation (per Erginbilgiç) is what *actually addresses* the trouble; restructuring may temporarily improve metrics without solving the underlying issue.

3. **Resilience-before-crisis as a corporate-finance principle.** Erginbilgiç's claim that the company could respond to 2025 tariff shocks at ~10× the capability of 2022 — attributed to pre-crisis capability building, not crisis-response improvisation — is a principle the wiki's academic papers don't directly address (they focus on prediction, not on capability building). This is a potential synthesis topic for a future thread.

**Limitations the authors acknowledge:**

- One-sided narrative (Erginbilgiç speaking about his own success). The interview format does not allow for counter-perspectives.
- Some of Erginbilgiç's claims are normative ("you need to be authentic") rather than empirical ("we did X and Y happened").
- The McKinsey case-study designation is mentioned by Lacqua but no detail given.

**Limitations not flagged:**

- **Cyclical aerospace recovery is not discussed.** Civil aviation widely rebounded post-COVID (2022–2024); a substantial share of Rolls-Royce's share-price recovery may be attributable to industry tailwinds rather than the specific transformation playbook. The interview does not engage this counter-explanation.
- **Defence-spending tailwinds.** UK + European defence budgets rose sharply 2022–2025 (post-Ukraine invasion). Rolls-Royce's defence segment is meaningful; some of the financial improvement may be defence-cycle-driven.
- **Selection bias on the playbook.** Erginbilgiç's "four pillars" are necessarily the things he *did*. We don't know what he *would have done* in a different industry / different starting condition, or whether the pillars generalise. Treating them as a portable playbook (the interview's framing for "what British companies can learn") requires evidence beyond a single case.
- **The "Rolls-Royce small modular reactor" segment** is presented optimistically but Rolls-Royce SMR is *not yet operational at commercial scale* — the test of the strategic-bet thesis lies ahead.

## Citations to chase

This is a primary-source interview, not an academic paper, so citations are sparse. Worth chasing:

- **McKinsey Rolls-Royce case study** — referenced by Lacqua as "the art of corporate transformation"; not located. Potentially the McKinsey Quarterly or Reflections series.
