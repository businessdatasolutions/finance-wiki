## Context (WHY)

The McKinsey Global Institute is McKinsey's flagship economics-research arm. Their "arenas" research programme has been published in multiple iterations (2024 and 2026 are the named releases); the 2026 update is positioned around the post-2022 AI boom as the dominant macroeconomic engine.

Two named concepts the report introduces / refines:

1. **"Arenas"** — industries where competition is structurally fluid (rapid technology shifts, scale economics dominant, value chains restructuring) and where outsized growth and outsized destruction co-occur. Distinct from the more familiar "industries" framing (which assumes structural stability over decades).
2. **"Omniscalers"** — companies that compete meaningfully across multiple arenas at scale. The report names 9 of them (likely the US hyperscalers + Chinese counterparts; the transcript references this set repeatedly but the exact list isn't enumerated in early segments and would require deeper transcript-mining to extract).

## Methods (HOW)

MGI methodology (per usual for their published reports):

- Top-down economic data — global revenue, growth-rate, market-cap, R&D-spending across industries.
- Bottom-up firm-level data — earnings, capex, segment reporting for the firms classified as omniscaler-candidates.
- Cross-arena pattern matching — what shared characteristics do the high-growth arenas have? What shared characteristics do the omniscaler firms have?
- Historical comparison — prior "arenas" iterations (2024 report) compared against 2026 to track which arenas grew, which decayed, which were newly added.

The video event itself is a recap + panel discussion; the underlying report (not ingested in this wiki) is the methodological work.

## Results (WHAT)

### The 8 arenas (from the description)

1. **AI services** — generative AI as a service (foundation models, agent platforms, fine-tuning, inference).
2. **Semiconductors** — the chip stack underpinning AI services; design + manufacturing + packaging + advanced lithography.
3. **Cloud infrastructure** — the hyperscale compute and storage platforms.
4. **Advanced manufacturing** — including the AI-enabled-manufacturing class and the reshoring + supply-chain reconfiguration trend.
5. **Robotics** — both industrial and humanoid robotics, with the latter accelerating since 2024.
6. **Next-generation energy systems** — including SMRs (small modular reactors), grid-scale storage, fusion-investment, and the renewables + AI-load infrastructure stack.
7. **Space** — commercial space, satellite constellations, launch economics.
8. **Biopharma** — including AI-discovered drugs, the GLP-1 (Ozempic-family) wave, gene/cell therapies.

### The "omniscalers" (9 firms — exact list not enumerated in the early transcript)

The transcript references the 9 omniscalers repeatedly. Reading the discussion: they're characterised as firms (a) competing meaningfully in 3+ arenas simultaneously, (b) investing at unprecedented capex scale (specifically: capex larger than most countries' GDP-defence equivalents), and (c) building cross-arena moats that single-arena specialists can't match. The transcript implies the list includes the US Big Tech players (Microsoft, Google/Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, possibly Nvidia, Apple) plus likely Chinese counterparts (likely Alibaba, Tencent, etc., though specific names not transcribed clearly).

*Note: an authoritative list would require either reading the actual MGI report or transcript-mining the panel segments more carefully. This source page surfaces the concept; the exact firm list is deferred to the report itself.*

### Cross-arena themes

- **AI as the foundation layer.** AI services + semiconductors + cloud infrastructure are described as a "stack" — value creation in the upper layers (AI services) depends on capacity built in the lower layers (chips, datacenters).
- **Geographic concentration.** US (West Coast) and China dominate; Europe is a meaningful participant only in semiconductors (ASML, IMEC), biopharma (Novo Nordisk, Roche), and selectively in robotics + energy.
- **Capex scale.** The omniscalers are investing capex larger than the GDP of mid-sized countries — fundamentally reshaping global capital allocation patterns.
- **"Race takes off" framing.** The 2024 MGI report named the arenas; the 2026 report describes the *velocity* — competition is intensifying rapidly, and the gap between leaders and laggards is widening.

## Discussion / Significance (SO WHAT)

For the wiki:

1. **The most data-dense McKinsey source in the corpus so far.** Unlike the recruiting-tone [[2026-05-19-sternfels-2026-mckinsey-leading-through-transformation|Sternfels webcast]] and the conversational [[2026-05-26-mckinsey-2026-leaders-not-knowing-it-all|Novak podcast]], this is research-derived content with named frameworks and specific firm-level claims.
2. **Cross-references the wiki's [[mission-protection-via-governance]] indirectly via Novo Nordisk.** Novo Nordisk is named as a likely omniscaler-adjacent firm in biopharma (the Ozempic-driven $600B valuation Ries 2026 also discusses). The biopharma arena is one of the few where European industrial-foundation governance has produced an outright winner.
3. **Adjacent to [[corporate-turnaround]] only loosely.** The "arenas" framing is competitive-strategy at the industry-restructuring level, not firm-distress-response at the individual-firm level. Different layer of analysis. Worth noting because future ingests of Bain / BCG / industry-specific frameworks might cluster against this MGI source.

**Limitations the source acknowledges:** the event is a launch + panel discussion; the underlying report has more data than the discussion can cover.

**Limitations not flagged:**

- **The "arenas" framing privileges the present winners.** Naming AI services + semiconductors as arenas in 2026 selects on the post-ChatGPT trajectory. The 2024 arenas list is referenced but the *churn* between 2024 and 2026 (which arenas dropped off, which were added) is not surveyed in this video.
- **Omniscaler concept is post-hoc.** The 9 firms exist; MGI named them omniscalers in 2026. Whether the omniscaler pattern *predicts* future winners (a forward claim) vs. *describes* present winners (a descriptive claim) is not separable from this discussion.
- **Geopolitical risk treatment is thin.** US-China decoupling is the most important variable for several of the named arenas (semiconductors above all); the panel mentions geopolitics but does not engage the risk-pricing systematically.
- **Capex scale claims need verification.** The "larger than mid-sized countries' GDP" framing is rhetorically strong; the actual capex numbers would benefit from being cited specifically.
